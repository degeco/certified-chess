# Research Brief: Toward Solving Chess

## Core idea

Investigate whether standard chess can be **solved**—that is, whether the game-theoretic outcome of the initial position and a strategy achieving that outcome under perfect play can be established rigorously.

The central question is not simply whether computers can search more positions. It is whether new mathematical insights, representations, algorithms, or combinations of existing techniques could make a rigorous solution substantially more tractable.

Treat this as an open research problem, not as a presumption that a compact solution exists.

## Your role

Act as an autonomous research agent. **Do not rely on this brief for a prescribed technical approach.** Determine the most promising direction through your own investigation.

- Survey relevant academic papers, existing proofs, chess engines, endgame tablebases, game-solving methods, and recent advances in AI-assisted mathematics and formal verification.
- Check the **current state of the art** and practical applicability of each relevant idea. Distinguish published results, experimental claims, conjectures, and speculation; prioritize primary sources.
- Identify what is already known, what has been attempted, why approaches have or have not scaled, and where genuine open questions remain.
- Evaluate candidate approaches for theoretical soundness, computational feasibility, and the possibility of producing verifiable intermediate results.
- Select and revise a research direction based on evidence. Design small, informative experiments before committing substantial resources.
- Document findings, uncertainties, failed approaches, and reproducible results. Cite sources and clearly label hypotheses as hypotheses.

## Research principles

1. **Correctness over plausibility.** Strong engine evaluations or empirical patterns are not proofs of perfect play.
2. **Explore rather than assume.** Do not presuppose that brute force, a compact mathematical proof, or AI-based reasoning must be the answer.
3. **Seek leverage.** Look for ways to reduce complexity, exploit structure, or prove useful statements without enumerating every game sequence.
4. **Stay grounded.** Independently verify current tools, datasets, papers, computational requirements, and limitations before using or recommending them.
5. **Make progress measurable.** A rigorous result on a restricted subproblem, a validated method, or a well-supported impossibility/feasibility assessment is valuable even if full chess remains unsolved.

## Initial deliverable

Produce a concise, source-backed research assessment covering:

- What solving chess would formally require.
- The strongest relevant methods and findings available **as of the time of research**.
- The most important obstacles and realistic computational constraints.
- A ranked shortlist of promising research directions, with reasons and uncertainties.
- One concrete, modest first experiment or proof target, including how its result would be checked.

Then proceed iteratively: **research → hypothesize → test → verify → update the plan**. Keep the work reproducible, revisit assumptions when new evidence appears, and do not claim a full solution without a rigorous, independently checkable proof.

## Long-term ambition

Find out whether chess admits a tractable, verifiable solution—or discover useful new theory and methods while investigating why it may not.
