### What Results Under Other Rules Do and Do Not Imply for `mini4x5`

A game-theoretic value established under one ruleset does **not** transfer to another ruleset unless every difference is shown to be unreachable or value-preserving. Comparing `SPEC.md` `mini4x5` against Hammersborg & Strümke (2022/2023) and the other variants in the table above shows three fundamental game-tree divergences:

1. **Immediate move-tree divergence at Ply 3 from pawn double-step and *en passant*:**
   On a 4×5 board with pawns starting on ranks 2 and 4 (`rqkr/pppp/4/PPPP/RQKR`), rank 4 is initially occupied by Black pawns and rank 2 by White pawns, so no double-step is possible on Ply 1. However, **as soon as any pawn vacates its starting square (by advancing to rank 3 and being captured, or by making a diagonal capture onto rank 3), the file becomes clear for the opposing pawn on that file to double-step directly to rank 4 (for White) or rank 2 (for Black)**.
   - *Concrete 3-ply proof of divergence (verified mechanically in [verify_survey.py](file:///home/dege/chess/01-survey/verify_survey.py)):*
     After `1. d2d3 c4d3` (`rqkr/pp1p/3p/PPP1/RQKR w - -`), both `c3` and `c4` are empty. Under Silverman (1981) and Hammersborg & Strümke (2022/2023) rules, White has the legal double-step move **`2. c2c4`** (which immediately attacks `b5` and `d5` and creates a protected passed pawn one square from promotion, while also enabling the *en passant* reply `2... b4c3` at Ply 4). Under `SPEC.md` `mini4x5` rules, **`2. c2c4` is illegal** (White can only play the single step `2. c2c3`, which walks into `2... d3c2`).
   - Because adding double-step and *en passant* adds legal moves for *both* the prover and the opponent at internal nodes of the AND/OR game tree, its effect on the game value is **non-monotone**: an added double-step can give White a winning attack that does not exist under single-step rules, or give Black a drawing counter-resource that fails under single-step rules. Therefore, a draw under double-step + *en passant* rules implies **nothing** about whether `mini4x5` without double-step/ep is a White win, a draw, or a Black win.

2. **Artificial draw inflation from Hammersborg & Strümke's 20-ply (10.5-move) no-progress rule:**
   In `patrik-ha/explainable-minichess` (`minichess/chess/fastchess.py`, line 183), `game_result()` declares an immediate draw whenever `self.ply_count_without_adv > 20` (i.e., after **21 plies / 10.5 moves without a pawn move or capture**):
   ```python
   if self.ply_count_without_adv > 20 or self.insufficient_material():
       return 0
   ```
   Under this 20-ply cutoff, any endgame or middlegame win that requires more than 10 moves of quiet piece maneuvering between captures or pawn pushes (for example, standard major-piece endgames on small boards; compare Kryukov's 3×4 and 4×4 tablebases where exact DTC reaches 26 moves = 52 plies and DTM reaches 43 to 73 moves) is **forcibly adjudicated as a draw**. Proving a draw under a 20-ply no-progress cutoff establishes only a *lower bound* on the draw region of the state space: a position that is a forced win in 12 quiet moves under `SPEC.md` (which has no 50-move or 20-ply rule) becomes a draw under `fastchess.py`. Conversely, proving a **draw under `SPEC.md`'s infinite-play / no-50-move rule** is strictly stronger against horizon-truncation artifacts: neither player can force checkmate in *any* finite number of moves, regardless of how many quiet maneuvering moves are allowed.

3. **Promotion-set differences (`Q, R, B, N` vs. `Q, R` or `Q`-only):**
   Because `mini4x5` starts with only `K, Q, R, P`, implementations such as Chelnokov's `Dagaz` (`Q, R` only) and `p4ralyn` / `mihapetr` (`Q` only) forbid underpromotion to a Knight (`N`) or Bishop (`B`). Under `SPEC.md` rules (and Hammersborg & Strümke's `fastchess_utils.py`), promotion to `Q, R, B, N` is legal. Promoting to a Knight (`N`) introduces a non-slider jump attack (capable of delivering check without blocking rays or avoiding stalemate traps where `Q` and `R` give stalemate), so a proof under `SPEC.md` rules must—and our `ATLEAST_DRAW w` and `ATLEAST_DRAW b` certificates do—cover all four promotion replies (`q, r, b, n`).

## 3. Verdict

- **Formal Verdict Category (from `TASK-3.md`):** **Published under different rules only** (with **no published proof or database under any rules**, and **no prior result found under matching `SPEC.md` rules**).
- **Precise Breakdown:**
  1. **Under matching `SPEC.md` rules (`rqkr/pppp/4/PPPP/RQKR w - -`, single-step pawns, no *en passant*, no castling, `Q/R/B/N` promotion, stalemate = draw, no 50-move rule):** **No prior result was found in our search.**
  2. **Under different rules for the same starting position (`rqkr/pppp/4/PPPP/RQKR w - -`):** Patrik Hammersborg and Inga Strümke (`arXiv:2211.05500v1`, Nov 2022; *IFAC-PapersOnLine* 56(2):9050–9055, Jul 2023) state in a single sentence in Section 2.1 that *"By building the full search tree, we have found that optimal play results in a draw for the Silverman 4x5 variants."* As verified by our direct inspection of their primary code repository (`patrik-ha/explainable-minichess`), their environment differs from `SPEC.md` `mini4x5` in three rules that alter the game tree starting at Ply 3: **(i) pawn double-step is enabled**, **(ii) *en passant* is enabled**, and **(iii) games are adjudicated as a draw after 20 plies (`ply_count_without_adv > 20`) without a pawn move or capture**. ## 4. Where It Would Belong

Because no prior solution exists under `SPEC.md`'s single-step / no-50-move-rule mini rules—and no checkable proof, certificate, opening line, or database has ever been published for `rqkr/pppp/4/PPPP/RQKR` under *any* ruleset—a concise research note presenting the certified weak solution of `mini4x5` (`6,107` White entries and `249,051` Black entries, independently checked by two zero-shared-code checkers) fits squarely in the established literature on solved small-board chess variants.

### Comparable Small-Game Solutions and Their Venues

1. **Mehdi Mhalla & Frédéric Prost (2013), *"Gardner's Minichess Variant is Solved,"* *ICGA Journal* 36(4):215–221 (`doi:10.3233/ICG-2013-36404`, preprint `arXiv:1307.7118v1`):**
   - **Comparable scope:** Weakly solved 5×5 Gardner minichess (`rnbqk/ppppp/5/PPPPP/RNBQK w - -`) as a **draw** under AISE single-step / no-castling / no-*en-passant* rules—the exact 5×5 rule specification adopted as `mini5x5` in `SPEC.md`. Like our `mini4x5` proof, their proof decomposes into two asymmetric draw oracles (one for White, one for Black), published as a 7-page *ICGA Journal* article (with a 16-page arXiv companion containing the explicit variation trees).
2. **Mark Watkins (2017), *"Losing Chess: 1. e3 Wins for White,"* *ICGA Journal* 39(2):123–125 (`doi:10.3233/ICG-170017`, accompanied by an archived 1.4 GB compressed proof tree and standalone technical report at the University of Sydney, updated 2019–2020):**
   - **Comparable scope:** A 3-page *ICGA Journal* Short Note announcing the weak solution of a chess variant via an explicit, downloadable AND/OR proof tree (`863,301,867` nodes, `66,187,848` transpositions). Notably, Watkins's public archival of the proof artifact enabled independent verification in 2019 that uncovered a 4-piece tablebase generator bug and led to a patched, re-verified certificate—directly illustrating the value of our `#cert v1` solver/checker separation.
3. **Roger Sayle (2024), *"Los Alamos Chess Game 2 (after P-K3) is Solved; Black Wins in 21 Moves,"* *ICGA Journal* 45(4):92–101 (`doi:10.3233/ICG-240247`):**
   - **Comparable scope:** A 10-page *ICGA Journal* article solving a historic reduced-board chess opening position (6×6 Los Alamos Chess without bishops, White starting without a Queen after `1. d3`), giving the exact forced value (Black mates in at most 21 moves / 41 plies starting with `1... c4`), the principal variation, and the search methodology.

*(Secondary/companion venues that regularly publish results of this scale include the Springer LNCS proceedings of **Computers and Games (CG)** and **Advances in Computer Games (ACG)**, and the **arXiv `cs.AI` / `cs.GT`** repository paired with a public proof-certificate Zenodo/GitHub archive.)*

### What Such a Note Is Expected to Contain

Based on the structure of Mhalla & Prost (2013), Watkins (2017), and Sayle (2024), a publishable short note (4–8 pages in *ICGA Journal* plus an open data archive) is expected to include:

1. **Exact Ruleset Specification & Literature Disambiguation:**
   - The formal board and starting FEN (`rqkr/pppp/4/PPPP/RQKR w - -`), explicitly contrasting `SPEC.md`'s single-step / no-castling / no-*en-passant* / no-50-move-rule specification (`mini4x5`, matching AISE minichess conventions) against Silverman's 1981 double-step problem (*CECV* p. 113) and Hammersborg & Strümke's (2022/2023) double-step + *en-passant* + 20-ply-cutoff RL environment.
   - The explicit caveat from `SPEC.md` Section 2 that omitting the 50-move rule defines an idealized infinite-play-is-draw game.
2. **State-Space & Game-Tree Context:**
   - Exact or estimated state-space size and branching factor of 4×5 chess, situating `mini4x5` between Silverman 4×4 (`mini4x4`, 5-entry `WIN w` mate in 4), Gardner 4×5 (`rnbk/pppp/4/PPPP/RNBK`), and Gardner 5×5 (`mini5x5`, $\sim 9 \times 10^{18}$ states).
3. **Solver Architecture & Independent Verification Protocol:**
   - How `05-solver` constructed the two invariant draw certificates (`ATLEAST_DRAW w` and `ATLEAST_DRAW b`), including handling of cycles and the `SPEC.md` v1.1 prover-stalemate coverage rule.
   - How two independently implemented, zero-shared-code checkers (`03-checker` in Python and `08-checker2`) verified every entry in `#cert v1` format, and why flat invariant certificates eliminate solver and horizon-cutoff trust assumptions.
4. **Quantitative Certificate Metrics & Asymmetry Analysis:**
   - Proof sizes (`6,107` prover-to-move positions for White's draw certificate vs. `249,051` prover-to-move positions for Black's draw certificate, a $\sim 40.8\times$ asymmetry), file sizes, solver runtime, and checker runtimes.
   - A brief game-theoretic explanation of why Black's defensive certificate is ~41× larger than White's (White moves first on a cramped 4×5 board with two Rooks and a Queen, forcing Black to defend against multiple sharp first-move pawn breaks and tactical sacrifices before stabilizing).
5. **Principal Variation & Human-Understandable Opening Defence:**
   - The main line of Black's defence (`1. d3 c3 2. Rd2 a3 3. b3 Ra4`, locking the queenside pawns and activating the `a5` Rook to `a4`) along with a summary table of Black's certified drawing replies against each of White's four legal first moves (`1. a3`, `1. b3`, `1. c3`, `1. d3`) and White's certified drawing move (`1. d3`) + replies to Black's responses.
   - Optionally, a comparison run of `05-solver` under Silverman's original double-step + *en-passant* rule (without Hammersborg & Strümke's artificial 20-ply cutoff) so the single note settles **both** rule variants with machine-checkable certificates.
